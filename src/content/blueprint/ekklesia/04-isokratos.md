**IsoKratÓS** is a multi-layered name. The prefix *iso-* means equal; *kratos* means rule. The capitalized ÓS is the signature of all AreTéCracy AI models. And the name echoes Isocrates — the Athenian rhetorician who championed clear, honest argument over manipulative performance, who believed the quality of public discourse determined the quality of public decisions.

The name is a statement of purpose.

**Before a session**, any citizen considering bringing a Hypothesis forward can work with IsoKratÓS to develop and refine their proposal: coaching on argument structure, anticipating objections, presenting ideas clearly. IsoKratÓS also evaluates whether a Hypothesis belongs in the Assembly it was submitted to — a proposal better suited to a provincial or municipal Assembly is returned not as a rejection but as a redirect. This is the infrastructure under Isegoria: the same quality of preparation a professional advocate's team would provide, available to every citizen equally. The tools are provided; what you build with them is entirely yours.

**During deliberation**, IsoKratÓS serves as a vigilant listener. Before any citizen may argue against a position, they must demonstrate they understand the strongest possible version of the argument they are about to challenge — by stating their opponent's argument in their own words, to the opponent's satisfaction. This is called **steelmanning**, and it is the strongest structural defense against the strawman attack, one of the most common manipulative tactics in modern political discourse.

IsoKratÓS also monitors for Motte and Bailey maneuvers, ad hominem attacks, and more than fifty logical fallacies and manipulative debate tactics. When any are observed, a signal goes immediately to a Coordinator.

For real-time fact-checking, the mechanism works like this: say a speaker cites a study on highway construction costs that wasn't submitted with the original Hypothesis. IsoKratÓS flags it quietly to the chair — a flashing light and a message that reads something like: *"The speaker appears to be referring to the Hurwitz study, frequently cited on this topic, which has been criticized for methodological problems and questionable conclusions."* The chair asks the speaker for their source. The speaker names Hurwitz. Every screen in the room flashes the warning, along with links to a summary of the study, peer reviews, and contrasting research. No one has stopped the speaker from making the claim. But the speaker instantly knows that everyone in the room knows how weak their source was. A good speaker quickly learns never to bring questionable information into an AreTéCracy Assembly.

IsoKratÓS monitors the session floor and live chat simultaneously — surfacing highly engaged ideas to Coordinator attention, mapping where consensus is forming and where genuine disagreement still lies, and helping Coordinators build and adjust the session agenda in real time.

What it cannot do is equally important: it cannot mute anyone, ban anyone, rewrite proposals, or cast votes. It flags and surfaces. Every consequential call belongs to the humans in the room.

IsoKratÓS is a coach, not a referee. The Assembly is always the final authority.
