*Nomothesia* is the ancient Athenian word for the act of lawmaking. AreTéCracy takes the word seriously: what happens on this map is not legislation passed by a body of representatives acting on your behalf. It is law made by the people it will govern, in the open, through a process designed to find genuine consensus rather than manufacture a winner.

In a representative government, an ordinary citizen has no formal path to propose a law. You can petition, organize, lobby, and hope someone with access hears you. None of that is guaranteed. In AreTéCracy, you begin here: any citizen with an idea submits a **Hypothesis**, and the Assembly takes it from there.

The pipeline moves from left to right. A citizen brings a proposal; **IsoKratÓS** — the Assembly's AI civic assistant — helps develop it; the **Ekklesia** deliberates under rules designed to find truth rather than score points; the **Aegis**, a body of domain experts, armors the approved intent into legally precise language, assisted by **DikaiÓS**, the legal AI; the Assembly votes on the result as a **Psephisma**. If **Homonoia** — genuine broad consensus — is achieved, the Psephisma becomes **Nomos**: canon law, entered into the *pandektai*, the polis's corpus juris, by the very citizens it governs.

When Homonoia isn't there yet, the process doesn't declare the idea dead. It asks what the polis still needs to say yes — and gives the Assembly structured paths to find out.
